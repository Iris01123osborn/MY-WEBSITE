Weekly Reflection

Week 2

Creating Websites

This is my first time to engage in a class form like workshop, so I prepared a lot. The online course in the minerva is helpful, I learned some basic knowledge about how to create websites and the effct of different code word.

Moving forward, I will continue to refine my HTML skills, expand website content progressively.
Overall, this session provided a clear, practical starting point for continuous web skill development across my studies.

Week 3

Web Scraping

This week's workshop introduced two ways of web scraping as a practical digital research method for collecting webpage data. Before the session, I finished the required reading about the legal, ethical and technical limits of scraping, and watched the introductory LinkedIn Learning video. I also installed the webscraper to prepare for class.

Through this week, I learned that web scraping can help gather materials such as online comments, social media content and media texts for digital media academic researchs. But scraping is not always permitted, and ethical risks need to be considered before collecting any data.

Week 4

Data Practices

In this class, I think the group task was the most useful part.We worked on the Reading literature scenario, looking at how Master students use generative AI in their studies. We followed the clear step-by-step process: first we decided what we wanted to find out, then we talked about ethical issues like academic integrity and voluntary participation, we looked for possible online data sources, and we thought about what information might be missing. I learned it is important to take this process seriously, instead of rushing straight to collecting numbers.

This image shows the content my group discussed in class.

Overall, this workshop gave me practical skills for constructing datasets, and it taught me to check where data comes from and who it might leave out.

Week 5

Data Visualisation

During this review of the dataset generation process, the most critical issue I encountered was that the data sources collected were mixed, and field standards were inconsistent. A large amount of unstructured information was difficult to classify and clean, which not only consumed a significant amount of processing time but also prevented the planned analysis from proceeding smoothly.

If we had more time for research, we would have further refined the types and sources of data and conducted surveys and in-depth interviews tailored to our research questions.

Through my studies on data visualization, I have come to realize that visualization is not merely a tool for presenting data, but also a crucial component of academic research.

Week 6

Identity, Algorithmic Identity, and Representation

Reading the We Are Data made me rethink algorithmic power over personal identity. I realise online traces do not only record who we are; systems actively assign identities that shape our opportunities. This reading warns against over‑trusting digital services. It encourages me to stay critical of how data profiling quietly constructs and constrains who we can become in contemporary digital society.

While conducting this research, I noticed that most of the content my friends posted was lifestyle, which suggests that our user profiles on Rednote are similar. I think this might also be why we became friendshhh.
